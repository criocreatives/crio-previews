Face Marketing · Billboard Concept

Turning the billboard into a landmark.

A sculptural architectural treatment developed to give a conventional digital billboard its own recognizable presence—without relying on the advertisement itself to make the structure memorable.

Concept Development

From visual reference to buildable language.

The starting point was not to redesign the billboard's engineering. The objective was to create a distinctive visual layer around a conventional support system: broader, relatively low in profile, expressive at night, and achievable without an unnecessarily complex custom structure.
01 · INSPIRATION

Movement through crossing light.

The stage-light reference established the core visual language: repeated diagonals, overlapping lines and a sense of energy created by convergence. The idea was translated from temporary event lighting into a permanent architectural feature rather than copied literally.

02 · SKETCH

Separating structure from expression.

The early sketch clarified the central idea: the screen remains a normal billboard element while the crossing members become a decorative layer around it. The treatment could extend beyond the screen's edges without becoming the primary structural frame.

03 · 3D STUDY

Testing proportion and repetition.

The first 3D study explored how a repeated X-pattern could frame the display. This established scale and fabrication logic, but the perimeter still read too much like a decorated rectangle rather than one coherent architectural gesture.

04 · REFINEMENT

Breaking out of the box.

The geometry was pushed beyond the screen envelope to reduce the rigid rectangular reading. From here, the concept evolved toward longer, more deliberate diagonal members with a clearer rhythm and fewer arbitrary intersections.

Design Choices

The refined system.

16:9 digital display

A standard, content-friendly proportion keeps the screen dominant and makes the overall structure more compact than the earlier wide study.

Solid black support wall

A dark architectural backing plane anchors the installation, conceals the conventional support system and gives the feature members stronger contrast.

Organized linear rhythm

The rods are arranged as a controlled sequence of opposing diagonals—not a random lattice—creating movement while retaining visual order.

Magenta + integrated light

The physical members provide colour in daylight, while selective linear illumination strengthens Face Marketing's roadside identity after dark.

Screen-first hierarchy

The architectural treatment deliberately surrounds rather than competes with the advertising surface. The message remains the primary focal point.

Fabrication-conscious design

The visual impact comes from repetition, angle, colour and lighting rather than an unnecessarily complicated load-bearing sculpture.

Refined Concept

The structure does not compete with the advertisement. It makes the place where the advertisement lives memorable.
